# ArtPoW

A free NFT every month on Polygon, claimed by mining a proof of work in the browser or directly on-chain.

- One mined claim per address and per month; anyone may submit a proof on behalf of its recipient.
- The difficulty follows a linear schedule of ~1000 tokens per month: every token minted ahead of schedule makes
  the next one 2^(1/10) harder, and time eases it back to the floor (2^28 hashes, about a minute in a browser).
  There is no cap: the supply regulates itself.
- Each month features a guest artist, who may receive an artist proof; the team receives a team proof. Proofs are
  minted without work, stay out of the numbering, the total and the schedule, and are independent of mined claims.
- Metadata is fully on-chain (`November 2026 • #256 / 1035`); images are referenced by URI (IPFS recommended).
- No royalties. The contract is not upgradable and its parameters are constants.

## Goals

- A free NFT every month, for an **open audience** (no existing community).
- Claims possible **100% on-chain** (for purists, without any website), and through a website that helps mining and
  then submits the transaction from the user's wallet.
- **The claimant always pays its own gas** (tiny on Polygon). No relayer, no paymaster.
- Tokens are **transferable and tradable**, **without any royalty**. The month's artist and the team are rewarded
  with one **proof** each (see [Artist and team proofs](#artist-and-team-proofs)).
- Role of the proof of work: **slow bots down**, make claiming **playful**, and **regulate supply** around
  **1000 tokens per month**, with no cap on the number (self-regulating system).
- Guiding principle: **keep it simple** (a single seed mechanism, no transaction to open a month).
- **Non-upgradable** contract (no proxy, no upgrade), **fixed parameters** (constants).
- Code and comments **in English** (published for audit).

## Design

### Network, standards, tooling

- Network: **Polygon** (very low gas).
- Standard: **ERC-721** (one unique token per claim).
  - ERC-1155 rejected: copies of a same `tokenId` are identical, which is incompatible with per-token metadata.
- **No royalties** (no ERC-2981): they have been optional on most marketplaces since 2023, and a fee taken on
  the work of unpaid artists would be badly perceived. ERC-721C (enforced royalties) rejected: it restricts free
  circulation.
- Metadata updates: **EIP-4906**.
- Tooling: Foundry + OpenZeppelin 5.

### Administration

- Deployed from an ordinary wallet, which becomes the first administrator and the first `owner()`.
- **List of administrators** (OpenZeppelin `AccessControl`): any administrator can add and remove others, so that
  losing one wallet does not lose the contract.
- **Two-step nomination**: an administrator **proposes** an address (`proposeAdmin`, which `cancelAdminProposal`
  withdraws); it becomes an administrator only by **accepting itself** (`acceptAdmin`). Sending that transaction
  proves the address is controlled on this network: no zero address, no typo, no multisig created on another
  network. Granting the role directly (`grantRole`) is disabled.
- **The last administrator** cannot be revoked and cannot renounce.
- Administrator powers: scheduling months, changing the team address, the `owner()` address, the collection logo
  and website, recovering assets sent by mistake (except tokens of this collection), managing administrators,
  shutting the contract down. They have **no power over holders' tokens**.
- Recovery of assets sent by mistake (also works after `shutdown`):
  - a plain POL transfer is **rejected** (no `receive` function); only a forced transfer (`selfdestruct`) can land
    here: `rescueNative`;
  - ERC-20 tokens: `rescueERC20`; NFTs of other collections received through `transferFrom`: `rescueERC721`;
  - **tokens of this collection can never be recovered** (`CannotRescueOwnTokens`): a token sent to the contract
    stays there for good;
  - ERC-1155: cannot land here (transfers are always "safe" and the contract rejects them).
- `owner()` (ERC-173 view): an address **set by the administrators** (`setOwner`, the deployer at first), used by
  marketplaces to decide who manages the collection page. It grants **no right** in the contract.
- Accepted risk: a single compromised administrator can revoke the others.
- Thanks to the two-step nomination, the last-administrator guard cannot be bypassed by appointing an address
  nobody controls.

### Shutdown

- **Irreversible** administrator function, meant for a discovered flaw (migration to a new, fixed contract).
- Stops for good: `claim`, `claimReserved` (proofs) and `scheduleMonth`. The running month is then considered
  **closed** (final total).
- Never blocks: transfers, approvals, `tokenURI`, `announceMonthTotal`, asset recovery.
- `shutdown` stays **immediate and available to a single administrator**: stopping by mistake or after a key theft
  is better than a contract that keeps running with a known flaw, or that a rogue administrator could keep from
  being stopped.

### Month calendar

- Administrator function: `scheduleMonth(month, start, end, content)`, with
  `content = (label, artist, artistAddress, image, animationUrl)`:
  - `label`: displayed name, in English (e.g. "November 2026"); a scheduled month does not have to match a
    calendar month;
  - `artist`: name of the month's guest artist (a different artist every month);
  - `artistAddress`: address receiving the artist proof (zero if the artist does not want one);
  - `image`: URI of the artwork; square is recommended for a consistent collection, not required;
  - `animationUrl`: optional (video, 3D, HTML); `image` then serves as the preview.
- The `MonthScheduled` event only carries the month and its dates (the content is read through `getMonth`), so that
  the bytes of an on-chain image are not paid twice.
- Rules:
  - months are scheduled **in order**, without skipping any;
  - duration **between 25 and 35 days** (`MIN_MONTH_DURATION`, `MAX_MONTH_DURATION`), `start` in the future, and
    `start ≥ end of the previous month` (no overlap);
  - **non-empty** `label` and `image` (artist name, artist address and animation are optional);
  - a month is **editable until it starts**, then **locked** (dates and content).
- Guards against administration mistakes: until a month starts, any date error (month too far ahead, gap,
  milliseconds instead of seconds) is fixed by scheduling it again; the duration bounds and the refusal of empty
  content prevent errors that would become final once it starts. A wrong image (wrong CID) cannot be detected:
  check off-chain before scheduling.
- A month ends at its scheduled end whatever happens: **no grace period**. If the next one is not scheduled, there is
  a gap without an active month (claims rejected).
- Good practice: schedule **contiguous** months (start of M+1 = end of M).
- Revealing the image early is fine (idea: an event on the website a week before).

### Monthly seed: accumulator chain (single mechanism)

- Seed of the first month: `blockhash(block.number - 1)` at deployment.
- Each month has an accumulator initialised to `keccak256(month seed, month number)` (at its first claim).
- Each claim updates the accumulator:
  `acc = keccak256(acc, blockhash(block.number - 1), recipient, nonce)`.
- **Seed of month M+1 = accumulator of month M**, frozen in practice at the end of M (no more claims are possible).
- No `openMonth()` function. A month without claims → predictable seed, accepted (a project nobody cares about has
  nobody to abuse it).
- Minor accepted risk, without change: the last claimant of a month (or the Polygon validator producing the last
  block) learns the next month's seed a few seconds before others and can start mining earlier. No notable
  consequence: the difficulty follows the schedule and a burst at the start of a month throttles itself.

### Claiming

- Signature: `claim(month, recipient, nonce)`, accepted when `start ≤ now < end`.
- Hash to provide: `keccak256(abi.encode(month seed, month, recipient, nonce)) < target`.
  - 128-byte message: **a single Keccak block**, hence one permutation per attempt.
- **The proof is judged against the target at the time of inclusion.** Time only makes it easier; only claims
  included in the meantime can make it insufficient (+7% difficulty per claim). The website mines with a
  **margin of 2 claims** (≈ 15% more work).
- **One mined claim per address and per month.**
- The recipient is part of the hash: a proof cannot be stolen, and anyone can submit it. Front-running only lets
  someone claim for itself, which stays within the rules of the game.
- Useful views: `activeMonth()` (open month, 0 otherwise), `monthSeed(month)`, `currentTarget(month)`,
  `targetAt(month, timestamp)`.

### Difficulty: monthly schedule

```
target(k, t) = floor × 2^((S(t) − k) / H)      capped at the floor
S(t)         = 1000 × (t − start) / (end − start)
```

- `k`: tokens already mined in the month; `S(t)`: number expected by the linear schedule.
- **H = 10** (`CLAIMS_PER_DOUBLING`): each token ahead of the schedule multiplies the difficulty by 2^(1/10) ≈ 1.07;
  10 ahead double it. Time eases it: halved every 10 × (duration / 1000) ≈ 7.2 h.
- Behind the schedule: difficulty at the **floor**. The delay **can be caught up** (deliberate choice: if many
  tokens remain at the end of a month, someone with computing power may take them).
- Every month **restarts from the floor**; no dependency between months. The actual month length is taken into
  account (1000 targeted in 28 as in 31 days).
- A burst throttles itself: an attacker 2^n times faster than the floor only gets about `10·n` tokens ahead of the
  schedule (≈ 160 for a GPU).
- The difficulty can **only** rise when tokens are minted ahead of the schedule.
- Floor: **2^28 hashes** on average (`FLOOR_DIFFICULTY_BITS = 28`), ≈ 1 minute in an average browser.

### Artist and team proofs

- Each month may have **one artist proof** (to the month's `artistAddress`) and **one team proof** (to
  `teamRecipient`), minted **without proof of work**.
- `claimReserved(month, edition)`: **callable by anyone** (the website or an administrator can trigger it as soon as
  the month starts); the token always goes to the designated address; only during the month, once per proof.
- `teamRecipient`: set at deployment, **changeable by administrators** (`setTeamRecipient`, consistent with the
  recovery logic in case a wallet is lost); zero = no team proof. A single address rather than one token per
  administrator (no incentive to multiply administrators).
- **Out of the numbering, the total and the difficulty schedule**; they do not touch the accumulator (an
  administrator cannot influence the next seed by choosing when to mint).
- They are **independent of mined claims**: the artist and the team may also mine a regular token (at most one extra
  proof for them). Since anyone can mine a claim on behalf of any address, this is what keeps a third party from
  blocking a proof by mining a regular token for the artist or the team first.
- Cost: ≈ 135,000 to 155,000 gas, paid by whoever triggers it.

### Token metadata

- `tokenURI` **generated on-chain**: base64-encoded JSON, no server; `image` and, when set, the month's
  `animation_url`.
- Name: `<label> • #<number>` (e.g. "November 2026 • #256"), then `<label> • #<number> / <total>` once the month is
  closed ("November 2026 • #256 / 1035"). Proofs: `November 2026 • Artist proof`, `November 2026 • Team proof`. The
  collection name is carried by the contract (`name()`).
- Attributes: `Month` (label), `Artist`, `Edition` (`Mined`, `Artist proof`, `Team proof`), then for mined tokens
  `Number`, `Total` (once closed, for every token), `Difficulty` (expected hashes at the target of the mint),
  `Leading zero bits` (leading zero bits of the hash, a rarity attribute). Proofs have no number, difficulty or zero
  bits.
- The total (mined tokens only) is read at call time: no closing write.
- `announceMonthTotal(month)`: **open to anyone**, once, after the month closes; emits `BatchMetadataUpdate`
  (EIP-4906) over the month's token range, proofs included (contiguous, since months never overlap), so that
  marketplaces refresh their cache.
- `external_url`: `<siteUrl>/token/<tokenId>`, present when the website is set.

### Collection metadata

- `contractURI()` (ERC-7572): on-chain JSON with `name`, `description` (fixed at deployment), `image` (logo) and
  `external_link` (website), the last two omitted when empty.
- `setCollectionInfo(logo, website)` (administrators): emits `ContractURIUpdated` and, since every token's
  `external_url` changes, `BatchMetadataUpdate` over all tokens.

### Image storage

- A stored string costs ≈ **980 gas per byte** (storage, JSON check, copy). An on-chain image as a `data:` URI
  (base64, +33%) costs ≈ 1.3 M gas per KB of PNG, and a transaction cannot hold more than ≈ 20 KB of image: only for
  pixel art or light SVG (native-resolution PNG wrapped in an SVG with `image-rendering: pixelated`).
- Arweave: one-time payment (≈ €0.04 per MiB on 31 October 2026); network running since June 2018; permanence relies
  on an endowment that assumes storage costs keep falling (risks: AR price, reliance on gateways). `ar://` is only
  documented by OpenSea; `https://arweave.net/<id>` works everywhere but depends on a gateway.
- **Chosen** (a different artist every month, from pixel art to photography): `image = ipfs://<CID>`, pinned by two
  services (or one service plus our own node), with a **systematic copy on Arweave** so that the same CID can be
  pinned again. Very light months: on-chain image possible case by case.
- Guidelines for artists: square recommended, from 1024×1024 to 4096×4096; PNG, JPEG, WebP or animated GIF; 20 MB maximum;
  pixel art at native resolution plus an upscaled version without smoothing; video, 3D or HTML through
  `animation_url` with a preview image.

## Tested edge cases

Expected behaviour of each test in `test/ArtPoW.t.sol`.

### Difficulty

1. **Starts at the floor**: at the start of the month, the target is the floor.
2. **One step per token ahead**: one token minted at the start → target × 2^(−1/10); ten → target ÷ 2 exactly.
3. **Time eases**: 10 ahead, then 5 schedule intervals → ÷ √2; 10 intervals → floor.
4. **Behind**: behind the schedule, the target stays at the floor.
5. **Self-limiting burst**: 40 tokens at the start → target ÷ 16 exactly.
6. **Proof judged at inclusion**: a proof valid when mined, outpaced by a claim
   included before it → **rejected**.
7. **Time never invalidates a proof**: without an intermediate claim, a proof stays valid later in the month.
8. **Actual month length**: same relative results for 28, 30 and 31 days.
9. **Independent months**: a very active month does not affect the starting target of the next one.
10. **Outside the month**: `targetAt` rejects a time outside the month.
11. **Monotonic (fuzz)**: the target never falls with time, never rises with claims, never exceeds the floor.

### Seed and accumulator

12. **Pre-mining**: one more claim in M changes the seed of M+1. Known and accepted windows: see
    [Security review](#security-review-31-october-2026).
13. **No paid opening**: the first claim of a month costs only ≈ 17,000 more gas (accumulator initialisation); no
    `openMonth()` function.
14. **Month without claims**: the seed of M+1 is the initial accumulator of M; the contract works normally.
15. **First month**: the seed is the `blockhash` of the block preceding the deployment.
16. **Proof from another month**: a proof computed with the seed of M is **rejected** for M+1.

### Calendar

17. **Next month forgotten**: M stops at its end; any claim is **rejected** until M+1 starts, then works.
18. **Gap between months**: the seed of M+1 does not depend on how late it is scheduled.
19. **Overlap**, **order**, **start in the past**, **duration outside [25 d, 35 d]** (edits included),
    **empty label or image** → **rejected**; durations of 25 and 35 days accepted.
20. **Edit before the start** (dates, label, artist, image) → accepted, without overlapping the next month.
21. **Lock**: any edit of a started month → **rejected**.
22. **Claim before the start** → **rejected**.

### Claims and security

23. **Double claim** → rejected; **two months** → accepted.
24. **Proof cannot be stolen**: a third party mints to the intended recipient; changing the recipient invalidates
    the proof.
25. **Submission by a third party** (sender ≠ recipient) → accepted.
26. **Transfer then claim** → rejected.
27. **Recipient unable to receive** (contract without `onERC721Received`) → rejected.
28. **Re-entrancy** from the receiver hook (`claim` and `claimReserved` re-entered) → consistent counters, numbers
    and ids.

### Administration and shutdown

29. **Two-step nomination** (no rights before acceptance, direct `grantRole` rejected, acceptance without proposal
    rejected, cancellation, proposing an existing administrator rejected, the zero address can never be appointed),
    **revocation**, **last administrator protected**, **non-administrator rejected** (including `proposeAdmin`,
    `cancelAdminProposal`, `setTeamRecipient`, `setOwner`, `setCollectionInfo`).
30. **Shutdown**: `claim`, `claimReserved` and `scheduleMonth` rejected; irreversible; transfers and metadata intact;
    running month closed and announceable.

### Proofs

31. **Triggered by anyone**, token to the artist and to the team address.
32. **Once** per proof and per month; **only during the month**; edition `Mined` rejected.
33. **No recipient** (zero address) → rejected.
34. **Independent of claims**: the artist and the team may also mine; a claim mined on behalf of the artist or the
    team by a third party does not block the proof.
35. **Out of the schedule, numbering and seed**: target, number of the first mined token, counter and next seed
    unchanged.
36. **Team address change** taken into account.
37. **Metadata**: names `Artist proof` / `Team proof`, `Edition` attribute, no number or difficulty; `Total` after the
    month closes, proofs not counted.
38. **Announcement**: the EIP-4906 range covers the proofs.

### Tokens and metadata

39. **Transferability**: standard ERC-721; ERC-721, metadata and ERC-4906 interfaces; no ERC-2981.
40. **Metadata during the month**: exact JSON, no total; two tokens of the same month differ.
41. **Metadata after closing**: name `November 2026 • #2 / 3` and `Total` attribute.
42. **`animation_url`**: absent from the JSON when empty, present with `image` as the preview otherwise.
43. **Total announcement**: rejected before the end; open to anyone; emits `BatchMetadataUpdate` over the right
    range; only once; empty month without EIP-4906 event.
44. **Unsafe strings** (quote, backslash, control character) in the label, artist, image or animation → rejected.
45. **Numbering**: restarts at 1 every month.
46. **Leading zero bits** exact (cases `0x80…`, `0x01…`, `0x00 80…`, `1`, `0`).
47. **Exp2**: exact powers, √2, monotonic (fuzz); **differential**: 300 `Exp2` vectors and 300 + 300 target vectors
    (easy and production floor) identical to the Python port.

### Collection, owner and active month

48. **`owner()`**: the deployer at first, changeable by administrators (ERC-173 event), with no right in the
    contract.
49. **`contractURI`**: exact JSON without and then with logo and website; `ContractURIUpdated` event.
50. **`external_url`**: absent without a website; `<site>/token/<id>` otherwise, with `BatchMetadataUpdate` over all
    tokens.
51. **Unsafe strings** in the logo or website → rejected.
52. **`activeMonth`**: 0 before the first month, during a gap and after the last one; the right month otherwise
    (inclusive start, exclusive end).

### Asset recovery

53. **Plain POL transfer** → rejected.
54. **Forced POL**, **ERC-20**, **NFT of another collection** → recoverable to the chosen address.
55. **Token of this collection** sent to the contract → not recoverable, it stays there.
56. **Non-administrator** → rejected; **after `shutdown`** → works.

## Measurements and simulations

### Hash rate and floor

- Miner `miner/src/pow.js`: Keccak-f in WebAssembly, one permutation per attempt.
- Measured (Node 22 / V8, the engine of Chrome, 2016 Xeon E5-1620 v4): **2 MH/s per thread**, 7.5 MH/s on 4 cores.
  Pure JavaScript (noble): 0.09 MH/s.
- "Average browser" assumption: **4 MH/s** median (phone ≈ 1–2 MH/s, recent laptop ≈ 10–15 MH/s).
- Floor 2^28 ≈ 2.7·10^8 hashes: ≈ 67 s on average at 4 MH/s (46 s median, exponential distribution).
- Reference GPU: RTX 4090 ≈ **5 GH/s** in single-block Keccak-256 (hashcat, mode 17800), about 1250 browsers.

### Simulations (`python3 sim/simulate.py`)

Chosen rule (H = 10, mining margin of 2), 5 runs per scenario:

| Scenario | Minted | People | Farms | People served | Median wait |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 300 people | 293 | 293 | 0 | 98% | 1 min |
| 3000 people (28, 30 or 31-day months) | 1054 | 1054 | 0 | 35% | 9 min |
| Launch: 2000 in 48 h, then 20/day | 680 | 680 | 0 | 26% | 2 min |
| 300 people + 64-core server | 1101 | 7 | 1094 | 2% | - |
| 300 people + 1 GPU all month | 1156 | 0 | 1156 | 0% | - |
| 300 people + 1 GPU from day 25 | 1157 | 240 | 917 | 80% | 1 min |
| 3000 people + 1 GPU for 2 h | 1054 | 973 | 81 | 32% | 9 min |
| 300 people + 10 GPUs all month | 1188 | 0 | 1188 | 0% | - |

Stale proofs (outpaced by another claim): ≈ 0 per month with the margin of 2, ≈ 1 without it.

Findings:

- Supply stays between ≈ 1000 and 1200 per month whatever the attackers' power (logarithmic dependency: 10 GPUs ≈
  +30 tokens compared with 1 GPU).
- **People cannot compete with a farm**: at the equilibrium set by one GPU (2^44 hashes), a browser would need ≈ 50
  days. Proof of work regulates supply, not the bots' share; only a proof of personhood could, which is outside the
  on-chain scope chosen here.
- A very popular launch consumes the schedule: the difficulty rises fast and people arriving during the rush give
  up; it comes back down by itself over time.

## Quality

- **Invariants** (64 sequences of 150 calls: claims, proofs, transfers, scheduling, time and administration): token
  ranges tile `1..totalMinted` month by month; mined numbers run `1..minted`, proofs have no number; one mined claim
  per address and per month; every administrator went through acceptance and one always remains; months are
  ordered, never overlap and last 25 to 35 days; the target never exceeds the floor.
- **Static analysis**: `forge lint src` reports nothing; the remaining findings are justified inline and in
  [`docs/static-analysis.md`](docs/static-analysis.md). The `block-timestamp` rule is excluded in `foundry.toml`:
  the calendar relies on timestamps by design.
- **Pinned compiler**: `pragma solidity 0.8.30` (optimizer 200 runs, EVM `cancun`), so that source verification on
  Polygonscan reproduces the deployed bytecode exactly.

## Security review (31 October 2026)

Full code review, with attack tests kept as regression tests where relevant.

- **Checked, no issue**: re-entrancy through the receiver hook (consistent state, permanent test); overflows; access
  control; `rescue` unable to move a token of this collection, even through an intermediate contract; JSON
  injection; bounded costs; precision of `2^x` (max error 0.0117%, monotonic); production floor identical bit for bit
  to the Python port (300 vectors, permanent test); no risky opcode (`DELEGATECALL`, `SELFDESTRUCT`, contract
  creation).
- **Proofs cannot be blocked**: they are independent of mined claims, so a claim mined on behalf of their recipient
  has no effect on them.
- **No uncontrolled administrator**: the two-step nomination (`proposeAdmin` / `acceptAdmin`) only admits addresses
  that sent a transaction on this network, so the last-administrator guard cannot be bypassed.
- **Accepted - pre-mining windows**: a month's seed is public before its start between deployment and month 1,
  during a gap between two months, and speculatively at the end of a quiet month. Possible burst at the start of the
  month, bounded by the schedule: ≈ 108 tokens (1 GPU, 3 h), ≈ 160 (1 GPU, 1 week), ≈ 242 (100 GPUs, 1 month). The
  project will be little known in month 1 and the following months will be contiguous.
- **Compromised administrator**: can neither touch holders' tokens nor mint more than 2 free tokens per month; can
  remove the other administrators, schedule a month with malicious content (permanent once it starts), redirect
  `siteUrl` (the link of every token), take over the collection page through `owner()` (and enable creator fees
  there, against the "no royalty" promise), stop everything. Mitigations: a multisig as the only administrator;
  alerts on `MonthScheduled`, `RoleGranted`, `RoleRevoked`, `ContractURIUpdated`, `OwnershipTransferred`,
  `TeamRecipientUpdated`; when in doubt, `shutdown` before the next month starts.
- **Off-contract points of attention**: wait for Polygon finality before mining on the next month's seed and before
  displaying numbers (reorganisations); check on Amoy that `MCOPY` and `PUSH0` are supported; smart wallets and

## Deployment

1. Store the deployer key in an encrypted keystore (never in `.env`):
   `cast wallet import deployer --interactive`
2. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` and fill it in: collection name, symbol and description, `TEAM_RECIPIENT`,
   optionally `COLLECTION_IMAGE` and `SITE_URL`.
3. Deploy and verify the source on Polygonscan (Amoy testnet first). The deployer becomes the first
   administrator and the first `owner()`. Always deploy with a plain `new` from the deployer wallet, never through a
   factory or CREATE2 proxy, which would become the administrator instead:

```shell
source .env
forge script script/Deploy.s.sol --rpc-url amoy --account deployer --broadcast --verify
# then on mainnet
forge script script/Deploy.s.sol --rpc-url polygon --account deployer --broadcast --verify
```

4. Further administrators (e.g. a multisig) are added with `proposeAdmin(account)`, then `acceptAdmin()` called
   by that account itself, which proves it is controlled on this network; direct `grantRole` of the administrator
   role is disabled. The deployer may then leave with `renounceRole` (the last administrator cannot).

If verification fails during deployment, it can be run again:

```shell
forge verify-contract <address> src/ArtPoW.sol:ArtPoW --chain polygon \
  --constructor-args $(cast abi-encode "constructor(string,string,string,address)" \
  "$COLLECTION_NAME" "$COLLECTION_SYMBOL" "$COLLECTION_DESCRIPTION" "$TEAM_RECIPIENT")
```
